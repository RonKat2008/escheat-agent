## Judge's 3-minute path

What a judge actually clicks, with the time spent and the reaction:

1. **`/` (0:00–0:20).** "Every uncashed check is a legal clock" plus the animated case file for check #10482. Wow: it is specific and states the problem in one line. They click **Open the demo**.
2. **Dashboard (0:20–0:45).** A huge "$61,915.00", the CA/TX/DE split, "Needs you 3", and a featured check with a five-date track. Wow: it looks like a real product. Risk: the "Next deadlines" sparkline is 90 days of grey ticks with one pink spike, so the eye slides past it. Nothing shouts that TX is already **late** (R2-02).
3. **Click the featured check, "View timeline" (0:45–1:15).** This is the best moment. Each date shows its arithmetic in plain English, plus the weekend roll-forward with a "counsel should confirm" note. A judge who reads one derivation believes the "the code decides the law" line. The risk is scroll fatigue: six dates, each with 5–8 citation chips.
4. **Chat (1:15–1:50).** "What do we owe and where?" gives a crisp exposure card with an "Offline replay" badge. Then they ask the next scripted question, "Draft the due-diligence letter for #10482", and get "Demo data changed since these answers were recorded" on a fresh seed (R2-01). **This is where a judge bounces.** It reads as a broken AI feature in the one place the pitch says is AI.

## Moments that feel flat (with specific ideas)

1. **Overdue has no voice.** Make "Late" the first block on the dashboard: a red strip such as "TX annual report: $773.74, 123 days late. Interest accruing (unverified rate)" with **Generate TX report** next to it. It is the most persuasive screen a finance buyer could see, and the data already exists (R2-02).
2. **Filing ends in silence.** After "Approve submission", type out the confirmation number character by character, animate the hero dropping from $61,915.00 to $60,340.90 with the `@number-flow/react` already in the bundle, and show a "−$1,574.10 filed" chip on the dashboard the next time it loads. Pin the evidence-pack download there.
3. **Recovery doesn't show its cleverness.** Seed one match whose recorded owner name is a variant ("NORTHWIND FIXTURES INC" or "Northwind Fixture Inc") and highlight the differing characters in "Matched as". Today the variant engine is invisible.
4. **Bulk actions feel like spreadsheets.** After a bulk draft, land on Approvals filtered to the new batch with a three-column board: "Ready (n) · Needs compose (n) · Blocked: why (n)". Clearing "Ready" should feel like inbox zero ("Nothing waiting on you. Next: 6 CA windows open Nov 1, 2027").
5. **Forecast is a good chart with no story.** Add a single line under the KPIs: "Your heaviest month is Apr 2027: 3 TX items ($5,725) cross dormancy." Highlight that bar on load. The chart-to-table cross-highlight is nice, but nobody finds it unless they're told it exists.
6. **The Next deadlines sparkline is empty space.** Replace it with a 12-month strip that shows DD windows as shaded bands (open to close) and report and remit dates as ticks, colored by state, with today's line. The close date (F26/R2-11) then becomes visible for free.
7. **Activity is a log, not a story.** Group by day and by subject ("Bayline Electric #10482: drafted 13:49 → composed → approved by Dana → sent offline 13:53"). Each item's history then reads as a chain of custody. Add a "Download audit pack for #10482" link.
8. **Rules review is close to great.** It now walks values one at a time. Add a per-state progress ring that fills as you approve, and after each approval show "This changed 21 items' dates" with a tiny before/after. Verification then has visible consequences.
9. **The demo clock's narrative still disappears.** The +1y toast ("12 × Watching → DD due, 2 × DD due → To report") is the best storytelling in the app and is gone in 4 s, before the page has even refreshed (R2-15). Keep a dismissible "What changed on Jan 30, 2028" panel on the dashboard, with each line linking to the filtered Items view.
10. **The command palette is navigation only.** Add verbs (R2-18). A judge who presses ⌘K and types "file" or "approve" should see the product act.
